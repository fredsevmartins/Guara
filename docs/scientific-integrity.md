# Scientific integrity

Stored claims may be wrong. Registered sources may be unreliable. Citations do not prove claims.
Value differences may reflect different contexts. Missing evidence does not establish falsehood.
AI-generated provenance must be explicitly supplied; it is never converted into verification.

Original wording is preserved exactly in each revision. Whitespace normalization is stored
separately and does not infer meaning. Relationships retain explicit actors, source locations,
and excerpts. Reviews and changes append history rather than deleting previous decisions.
Accepted-for-use means a reviewer recorded a decision, not objective truth. Actor strings are
self-attributed and not authenticated. Do not fabricate reviewer identities.

Uncertainty lives in context, provenance, INCONCLUSIVE links/reviews, insufficient-evidence
summaries, and insufficient-comparability outcomes. No confidence score is invented. Verification
statuses reflect implemented checks only: source registration never verifies a URL/DOI or paper.
